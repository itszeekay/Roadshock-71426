1.3
System Power Modes
There are five typical power modes in ASR560X, the ACTIVE, TX, RX, IDLE and deep sleep
(DS-X). The SoC boots up and enters into the ACTIVE mode when it is powered on. The
ACTIVE mode is the basic mode of this Soc. All other modes can only start from the ACTIVE
mode and switch back to the ACTIVE mode. During the communication process, the soft stack
requires the SoC to enter into the receiver (RX) or transmitter (TX) mode from the ACTIVE
mode. If there is no TX or RX activity in a relatively short time, the chip will go to the IDLE mode
to save power, in which the CPU enters into WFI mode and all the peripherals are suspended.
If the spare time is long enough, the SoC will jump from the ACTIVE to the deep sleep (DS-X)
mode to extremely save power consumption. It may be waked up by the GPIO events, internal
time alarms or BLE anchor point requirement and then returns back to the AC · VE mode.

AUDIO-IN Path
The audio-in path is a high-performance analog to digital converter for differential microphone
or line-in input. It consists of a programmable front-end filter (FE filter), a programmable gain
amplifier (PGA), a continuous-time sigma-delta AID converter and a microphone bias. The
audio-in source can be either a microphone signal with a full-scale level as low as 16 mVppd
or a line-in signal with up to 2 Vppd signal swing with different external ac-coupled capacitors
(4.7 nF for MIC-in mode or470 nF for LINE-in mode). The microphone bias provides the voltage
varying from 1.5 V to 2.2 V (VBAT must 0.3 V higher than the output voltage) for the external
microphone with at least 80 dB PSRR over the audio band (20 Hz to 22 KHz).
